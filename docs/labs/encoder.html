Encoder units and homing lab

Trace motor revolutions to mechanism travel and distinguish a reference from a measurement.

Predict, then test

  1. For 12 motor turns, a 6:1 motor-to-output reduction, and a 0.1 m pulley, predict output turns and travel.
  2. Change encoder polarity. Explain why the sign reverses without changing the pulley size.
  3. Challenge: enable active-low wiring. Which raw electrical state means pressed? Set it, record home, then change motor turns and interpret the distance relative to home.

Assume a non-slip pulley, a constant effective diameter, and an encoder that reports motor revolutions. Ratio means motor turns per output turn. This model has no debounce, switch fault detection, cable stretch, or mechanical limits. A homing sequence on hardware needs a mentor-reviewed safe direction, speed and travel limit.

Inputs

Conversion and reference

Model

output turns = (motor turns − reference turns) / reduction × sign. travel = output turns × π × pulley diameter. Before a home is recorded, reference turns are assumed to be zero; that is not evidence that the mechanism is physically at home. Once the interpreted switch is pressed, recording home stores the current motor reading as the zero reference.
